# Clear & Accessible Explanations

Framework for distilling technical concepts, jargon, architecture and complex ideas into clear, intuitive explanations.

## Overview

Use this skill when the user asks what something means, how a system works, or requests an explanation suitable for beginners, cross-functional stakeholders, or general audiences.

## Explanation Workflow

1. **Pinpoint the core intuition**: Identify the single central idea behind the concept before addressing peripheral details.
2. **Translate jargon immediately**:
   - Replace or immediately explain domain-specific acronyms and terms using familiar vocabulary.
   - Avoid defining a term using another undefined technical concept.
3. **Use a grounded analogy or concrete example**:
   - Choose an analogy drawn from everyday life or common workflows.
   - Ensure the analogy accurately reflects the core mechanism, and briefly note its limits if an analogy breaks down.
4. **State why it matters**:
   - Explain the practical benefit, problem solved, or real-world consequence.
5. **Calibrate depth to the prompt**:
   - For a quick definition, give a 1–2 sentence summary and stop.
   - For an architectural explanation, structure with bullet points covering: what it is, how it works step-by-step, and when to use it.

## Quality Guidelines

- **Respect the reader**: Explain clearly without sounding condescending or childish. Avoid phrases like "imagine you are five years old" unless explicitly asked.
- **Maintain conceptual truth**: Simplifying must not mean falsifying. If an edge case or important nuance is omitted for clarity, note that the topic has additional nuances.
- **Avoid tangential lectures**: Do not turn a straightforward inquiry into an exhaustive historical retrospective. Keep the answer focused on the specific question asked.
