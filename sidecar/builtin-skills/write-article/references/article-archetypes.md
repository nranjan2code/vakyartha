# Article Archetypes & Editorial Guidelines

Reference guide for tailoring structure, tone, pacing, and visual layout across common article genres in Vakyartha.

---

## 1. News Analysis & Investigative Explainer

- **Audience**: Informed professionals, decision-makers, and curious general readers seeking the "why" and "what next" behind current headlines.
- **Tone**: Objective, authoritative, sharp, and evidence-grounded.
- **Core Structure**:
  1. **Headline & Subdeck**: Clear, timely hook capturing the event and its primary implication.
  2. **The Lede**: Inverted pyramid style; answer who, what, when, and the stakes in the opening paragraph.
  3. **Key Takeaways Box**: 3–4 bulleted points highlighting immediate facts, key numbers, and next milestones.
  4. **The Catalyst & Background**: Contextualize the event—what occurred, historical precedents, and key players.
  5. **Impact on Key Stakeholders**: Break down effects on major corporations, startups, regulators, or consumers.
  6. **Data & Evidence**: Comparative metrics, revenue or capex tables, and stat cards.
  7. **Dissent & Alternative Views**: Industry counterarguments, skeptical analyst perspectives, or policy concerns.
  8. **Outlook & What to Watch**: Specific dates, pending regulatory approvals, or upcoming quarterly reports.
  9. **Sources & Verification**: Documented publication links, filings, and retrieval dates.

---

## 2. Thought Leadership, Opinion Column & Persuasive Essay

- **Audience**: Industry peers, executives, policymakers, or domain thinkers looking for fresh frameworks and arguments.
- **Tone**: Convincing, thoughtful, provocative yet intellectually honest.
- **Core Structure**:
  1. **The Hook**: A vivid opening observation, paradox, counterintuitive data point, or personal anecdote.
  2. **The Central Thesis**: Explicit statement of the core argument within the first three paragraphs.
  3. **Core Argument Arc**:
     - *Pillar 1: The Status Quo / Prevailing Myth* (Why conventional wisdom falls short).
     - *Pillar 2: The Structural Shift* (Underlying technological, economic, or cultural forces driving change).
     - *Pillar 3: The New Paradigm* (The thesis illustrated with concrete case studies or data).
  4. **Steelmanning the Counter-Argument**: Seriously consider and refute the strongest opposing objection.
  5. **Pull-Quotes**: Emphasize pivotal assertions using prominent callouts or quote blocks.
  6. **Call to Action / Synthesis**: A forward-looking mandate or open philosophical question for the reader.

---

## 3. Academic / Analytical Journal Article & Whitepaper

- **Audience**: Researchers, engineers, technical leads, analysts, and domain specialists.
- **Tone**: Formal, rigorous, methodical, precise, and qualified.
- **Core Structure**:
  1. **Title & Abstract / Executive Summary**: Comprehensive summary of problem statement, methodology, primary findings, and practical implications.
  2. **Metadata**: Author, institutional/organizational affiliation, date, version, and license.
  3. **Introduction & Motivation**: Scope of inquiry, existing literature/benchmarks, and problem definition.
  4. **Methodology & Assumptions**: Datasets evaluated, sample boundaries, metrics, and experimental or analytical setup.
  5. **Results & Empirical Findings**: Granular breakdown supported by structured tables (`table`), charts (`chart`), and stat badges.
  6. **Discussion & Implications**: What the results mean in practice; operational, architectural, or commercial tradeoffs.
  7. **Threats to Validity & Limitations**: Explicit disclosure of unmeasured variables, edge cases, and confidence intervals.
  8. **Conclusion & Future Work**: Key milestones and unanswered questions.
  9. **References**: Formal bibliographical citations with DOI/URL attribution.

---

## 4. Feature Story & Narrative Journalism

- **Audience**: General readers interested in rich storytelling, human drama, and behind-the-scenes accounts.
- **Tone**: Evocative, character-driven, immersive, yet strictly factual.
- **Core Structure**:
  1. **Hero Imagery**: Engaging visual banner or photo gallery establishing scene and mood.
  2. **Narrative Scene Lede**: Open in the middle of the action (*in media res*), setting, or a pivotal character moment.
  3. **The Nut Graph**: 2–3 paragraphs stepping back from the opening scene to explain the broader historical or cultural significance.
  4. **Narrative Chapters**: Use subheadings to guide readers through chronological or thematic chapters.
  5. **Vignettes & Direct Quotes**: Integrate dialogue, on-the-record statements, and situational details.
  6. **Visual Pacing**: Intersperse narrative prose with photo galleries (`gallery`), pull-quotes (`quote`), and character profiles.
  7. **Kicker**: Concluding image or resonant quote that circles back to the opening scene.

---

## 5. Technical Deep-Dive, Explainer & How-To Guide

- **Audience**: Developers, practitioners, system administrators, and technical enthusiasts.
- **Tone**: Pragmatic, instructional, lucid, and step-by-step.
- **Core Structure**:
  1. **Overview & What You Will Learn**: Concise summary of what is being built or explained.
  2. **Prerequisites & Assumptions**: Tools, versions, baseline knowledge, and system requirements.
  3. **Conceptual Mental Model**: High-level architectural overview or workflow diagram before diving into code.
  4. **Step-by-Step Implementation**:
     - Numbered sections for each distinct phase.
     - Formatted code blocks (`code`) with explicit language identifiers and inline comments.
     - Callouts for common pitfalls or configuration errors (`alert` with tone `warning`).
  5. **Verification & Testing**: Commands and expected outputs to verify that the implementation works.
  6. **Performance, Security & Production Best Practices**: Operational considerations for scaling or deploying.
  7. **Summary & Reference Links**: Cheat sheet, GitHub links, and official documentation references.

---

## 6. Digital Publication, Newsletter & Social Essay

- **Audience**: Busy subscribers, mobile readers, and newsletter communities (Substack, Medium, company blogs).
- **Tone**: Conversational, engaging, punchy, and highly scannable.
- **Core Structure**:
  1. **Compelling Subject / Headline**: Direct, curious, or benefit-driven.
  2. **Reading Time & Date**: Byline badge (e.g. `⏱ 4 min read • Nov 2026`).
  3. **"TL;DR" Executive Brief**: 3 bullet points summarizing the entire post in an `alert` box.
  4. **Short Sections with Bold Takeaways**: Limit paragraphs to 2–3 sentences.
  5. **Visual Anchors**: Embedded charts, screenshots, stat metrics, or memes/illustrations.
  6. **Interactive Reader Prompt**: Conclude with a question or discussion trigger inviting reader comments.
